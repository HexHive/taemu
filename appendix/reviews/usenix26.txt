Review #633A
===========================================================================

Impact & Reasons to accept
--------------------------
(1) The paper studies shared-memory double-fetch (TOCTTOU) vulnerabilities in Trusted Applications (TAs). This is an underexplored but widely deployed attack surface in TEE systems.

(2) Results across several major commercial TEEs suggest that the problem is not isolated and can affect real-world deployments.

Limitations to add
------------------
(1) Manual harness construction is required.

(2) The evaluation covers a limited number of TAs.

Constructive suggestions to improve the paper
---------------------------------------------
(1) The paper would benefit from a clearer explanation of what is fundamentally new compared to prior work on double-fetch detection and fuzzing. It would help to explain why existing approaches can not directly be appled in the TEE setting.

(2) The evaluation relies on manual harness construction ("we manually analyze each TA and write a fuzzing harness"), which may limit scalability. It would help to discuss how this affects coverage and the likelihood of missed vulnerabilities.

Reasons to not accept the paper
-------------------------------
(1) The technical novelty is somewhat limited. The system combines existing ideas such as fuzzing, memory access tracing, and snapshot-based analysis, and the main contribution lies in applying them to the TEE setting.

(2) The evaluation is limited in scale. The analysis relies on manual harness construction and covers a relatively small number of TAs, which may affect generality.

Questions for the author response
---------------------------------
(1) The current approach relies on manual harness construction for each TA. How do you expect the system to scale to larger datasets, and what is the feasibility of automating this step?

(2) The system introduces a three-stage design (exploration, snapshot fuzzing, and distillation). What are the key aspects that are fundamentally new compared to prior double-fetch detection approaches, rather than adaptations to the TEE setting?

Overall merit
-------------
2. Weak reject

Review #633B
===========================================================================

Impact & Reasons to accept
--------------------------
+ Comprehensive evaluation
+ Previously unknown vulnerabilities found

Limitations to add
------------------
None

Constructive suggestions to improve the paper
---------------------------------------------
The paper claims that the proposed exploration/double fetch snapshot fuzzing/distillation sequence (aka ScHMuzz) serves as an oracle for double-fetch vulnerabilities, but I am not fully convinced. The approach in the distillation phase replays a crashing input with and without double-fetch fuzzing and treats any discrepancy as evidence of a double-fetch bug. However, this may not hold in all cases. For instance, consider a scenario in which the first and second fetches are related but independent operations with "separate" validity checks, and a crash results from an incorrect second check rather than a true double-fetch race condition. I am not sure whether such cases satisfy the definition of a double-fetch vulnerability as formulated by DEADLINE. Obviously, bugs arising from general nondeterminism could manifest during replay as well, further undermining the reliability of the oracle.

At its core, the proposed technique is a form of snapshot-based fuzzing: the trusted application (TA) is snapshotted after the first fetch and then resumed with modified shared memory to simulate an overlapping second fetch. Beyond the manual effort required to write harnesses, I could not identify significant technical contributions beyond this (especially those specific to the TrustZone TA environment). The paper would be considerably strengthened by proposing techniques that automate or reduce this manual harness-writing burden.

On the positive side, the evaluation setup and real-world validation is impressive. The work targets TAs across nearly all major TEE platforms, and the reproduction of findings on real devices and phones demonstrates strong practical relevance. It was also interesting to see many TAs/TEEs support zero-copy memory and there are many overlapping fetches.

Reasons to not accept the paper
-------------------------------
- Snapshot-based double fetch fuzzing is rather straightforward
- A lot of manual efforts required

Questions for the author response
---------------------------------
1. Could you clarify whether the proposed sequence constitutes an oracle? A more in-depth discussion of false positives and negatives would be helpful.

2. Could you share the status of responsible disclosures?
